Comparison & conclusion

Who controls the city?

Agency matters, but choices are made within unequal conditions.

Film still: Burning (Lee Chang-dong, 2018), reproduced by Flickering Myth.

Not simply passive victims

The disadvantaged characters still act. The Kims seek better-paid work, Miso rejects a conventional lifestyle, and the Yongsan residents resist eviction. Their actions prevent the films from presenting poorer people as powerless. However, agency does not remove the conditions around them. High housing costs, insecure work and unequal political influence continue to limit the choices available.

Four ways of seeing

The films use different methods. Parasite and Burning build suspense through visual contrast and uncertainty. Microhabitat focuses on ordinary encounters, while The Remnants gives survivors time to speak about a specific conflict. Together, the films move between metaphor, daily experience and documented history.

Counterargument

Can inequality become entertainment?

Parasite can be criticised for using humour, suspense and carefully designed images to make poverty entertaining for a global audience. Kim Kyung Hyun’s discussion of Korean cinema in the global era helps frame this tension between local social criticism and international appeal. Yet the film’s popular style also draws viewers into its class conflict. The flood sequence makes the material consequences of inequality difficult to dismiss. Its accessibility therefore strengthens the film’s reach, even while raising questions about spectacle.

Final answer

Urban progress is not only a skyline

These films question whether progress should be measured only through economic growth and new buildings. Parasite shows how housing shapes everyday comfort and vulnerability. The Remnants records what redevelopment costs those displaced by it. Microhabitat and Burning add the frustrations of people whose work, housing and social visibility remain uncertain. A prosperous city, they suggest, should also be judged by whether residents can live securely and have some control over their future.

The city is not equal simply because everyone can see the same skyline.
